# Trade Closing Walk-Through — Pain-Point Close

Use this on contractor sales meetings when you're walking a trade through the system and the objections start. The structure is simple and repeats for every pain point:

1. **Ask the pain question.** (They'll say "no.")
2. **Ask "Why not?"** — then shut up and let them explain. Their answer is the pain.
3. **Bridge to the tool.** "Well, that's exactly why we have *this* right here." Show it on screen.

Run all four pain points, then go to the ROI close, then the scarcity close.

---

## Pain Point 1 — Close Rate

> **You:** "Are you closing over 70% of the meetings you're going to?"
>
> **Them:** "No."
>
> **You:** "Why not?"
>
> **Them:** *[their reason — price shoppers, slow follow-up, homeowner went with someone else, etc.]*
>
> **You:** "OK, I hear you. But myself and my clients are closing 70% — and it's because of this tool right here."

➡️ **Show:** the full system — booking, instant proposals, pricing calculator, website/online presence.

---

## Pain Point 2 — Proposal Speed

> **You:** "Are you sending your estimates and proposals within an hour after your meeting with the client?"
>
> **Them:** "No."
>
> **You:** "Why not?"
>
> **Them:** *[their reason — have to go back to the office, do takeoffs at night, it takes days to price, etc.]*
>
> **You:** "Well, this tool right here does exactly that."

➡️ **Show:** the proposal/estimate tool — proposal out the door within an hour of leaving the house.

---

## Pain Point 3 — Tire Kickers & Wasted Drive Time

> **You:** "Are you driving out to estimates and consultations and people end up being tire kickers and wasting your time?"
>
> **Them:** "Yeah."
>
> **You:** "Why is that? Aren't you qualifying them with a pricing calculator so they know what to expect on price *before* you drive an hour each way?"
>
> **Them:** "No."
>
> **You:** "Well, that's why we have it right here."

➡️ **Show:** the pricing calculator — homeowner sees a realistic range up front, so the only people booking are people who can afford the job.

---

## Pain Point 4 — Trust Before You Walk In

> **You:** "Are homeowners hesitant to make a decision when you show up to the estimate? Or do they already know they trust you and that you're the company they want to go with?"
>
> **Them:** "They're hesitant." / "No, they don't already know."
>
> **You:** "Why not?"
>
> **Them:** *[their reason — thin website, few reviews, no real online presence, they're one of five bids, etc.]*
>
> **You:** "Well, that's exactly what this website and everything we do with your online presence is built for. By the time you knock on the door, they've already picked you."

➡️ **Show:** the website, reviews, and online presence work.

---

## The ROI Close

> **You:** "For **[$X]** with our packages, you get every tool I just showed you.
>
> Now — if this lands you one single bathroom or basement… let's say a basement at **$150,000**, and because of these tools you were able to price it at **20% more margin** and you won that deal *specifically* because of these tools —
>
> is it not worth it to you to make your **entire investment back on the first single job**? Everything after that is straight profit — on top of the profit you already made on that first job."

**Quick math to have ready:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Job size | $150,000 basement |
| Extra margin from these tools | 20% → **$30,000** |
| Package investment | **[$X]** |
| Paid back on | **Job #1** |

---

## The Scarcity Close (No Hard Close)

> **You:** "Look, I'm not here to hard close you. You and I absolutely do not need your business. After this call I'm going to talk to your competitor — and then *you* will be competing against all of this.
>
> And I promise you, if you're already struggling to win deals now, just think about trying to compete against the company that has all of this.
>
> If you were the homeowner, who would you feel most comfortable doing business with?
>
> - Someone who shows up **unorganized and unprepared**, gets estimates back in **4–5 days**, with **slow follow-up and communication**…
> - …or a company that **has their stuff together** and is a **very clear, safe choice** for a homeowner to make this investment with?
>
> Who would you pick?"

Then stop talking. Let them answer.
